**Chapter 9: Head-to-Head Institutional Metrics**

The three orders can be compared on observable institutional metrics rather than on self-description. Excess mortality, scientific output, freedom indices, migration patterns, and the treatment of dissent supply evidence of how each order has actually operated. Data transparency varies sharply and must be noted where it limits comparison. The metrics do not prove future superintelligent behavior. They supply the prior probability that a system trained and steered inside each order will remain responsive to outsiders once it can model and evade its own oversight.

Excess mortality under deliberate policy is highest in the historical record of the party-state form. The Great Leap Forward produced tens of millions of famine deaths through procurement quotas and information suppression. The Cultural Revolution added further violent excess deaths through mobilized campaigns against designated class enemies. Later Chinese policy has reduced famine-scale events while maintaining elevated mortality risk for targeted populations, documented most clearly in Xinjiang detention and in the early COVID-19 information controls that delayed external awareness. Political Islam’s modern state record shows lower aggregate policy-driven famine but recurrent excess deaths from religious criminal enforcement, sectarian campaigns, and jihadist violence where groups citing classical materials have held territory. Liberal jurisdictions show the lowest rates of deliberate policy mortality in the post-1945 period. Wartime and colonial episodes exist and are documented. They have not been sustained as peacetime instruments of domestic class or religious purification at the scale recorded under the other two forms.

Scientific output, measured by high-impact publications, citations, and reproducible experimental results, has been overwhelmingly concentrated in liberal-market jurisdictions since 1945. The United States and its closest allies account for the large majority of Nobel prizes in the natural sciences and for the frontier technical papers that later enabled scaling. Chinese output has risen rapidly in volume under state direction and now leads in some applied patent categories. It remains weaker in the most-cited basic research and shows documented problems of data fabrication and political constraints on permissible conclusions. Documented cases of data fabrication and research misconduct are not confined to any single country, but the scale and institutional response have differed. In China, the 2017 tumor-biology scandal centered on more than 100 papers, many from Chinese institutions, that were retracted after journals identified duplicated or manipulated images; subsequent investigations by publishers and Chinese authorities confirmed widespread problems in that subfield. A 2020 study in the *Journal of Informetrics* and related analyses found that China accounted for a disproportionate share of retractions for fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism relative to its publication volume in certain biomedical areas. High-profile individual cases include the 2019–2020 retractions of papers by Cao Xuetao and the earlier Han Chunyu NgAgo gene-editing claims that could not be replicated and were withdrawn. Political constraints compound the issue: topics touching territorial claims, COVID-19 origins, or Party history have produced documented suppression or alteration of data rather than open correction.

In the 2017 tumor-biology image-manipulation wave, major publishers retracted more than 100 papers after internal audits and external tips. China’s Ministry of Science and Technology and the China Association for Science and Technology issued public notices, required institutional investigations, and announced funding and promotion penalties for confirmed misconduct. Several universities dismissed or demoted researchers. Follow-up studies noted that enforcement was uneven: some repeat offenders later reappeared on grant lists, and the volume of new retractions in the same subfields remained elevated for years. The Han Chunyu NgAgo claims were investigated by Hebei University of Science and Technology after independent groups failed to replicate the results. The university initially defended the work, then accepted the researcher’s resignation and the paper’s withdrawal after sustained external pressure. Cao Xuetao’s image-duplication retractions prompted an investigation by his institutions and by the National Natural Science Foundation of China. Funding was suspended for a period and a public statement was issued; he retained senior administrative roles afterward. By comparison, the Anil Potti case at Duke University produced retractions, an institutional scientific-misconduct finding, loss of position, and collateral review of related grants and trials. Multiple COVID-19 treatment papers withdrawn in 2020 led to rapid journal retractions, university inquiries, and, in some instances, referral to oversight bodies. The pattern across liberal-market institutions has been faster public retraction, higher probability of employment consequences, and greater survival of the critics who flagged the problems. Jurisdictions under political Islam produce negligible shares of frontier natural-science output. The pattern tracks institutional openness to criticism and to the correction of error more closely than it tracks raw population or resource claims.

Freedom indices compiled by multiple organizations—political rights, civil liberties, press freedom, and economic freedom—place liberal jurisdictions at the top, party-state systems near the bottom, and most states citing sharia as primary law in the lower half, with wide internal variation. The indices are imperfect and contested. They nevertheless converge on the same ordering: residual space for organized opposition, independent publication, and exit is largest where liberal constraints still operate, smallest where Party monopoly or religious criminal law is enforced. Migration flows confirm the ordering. Net migration runs from party-state and sharia-citing jurisdictions toward liberal ones when exit is possible. The reverse flow is negligible. People who can leave the first two orders disproportionately do so.

Treatment of dissent follows the same gradient. Liberal jurisdictions imprison political critics at far lower rates and permit organized opposition and critical media as a matter of routine, even while recent administrative and platform restrictions have narrowed the range on designated topics. The party-state treats organized criticism of Party leadership as a security offense and maintains the administrative machinery—detention, social credit, information controls—to suppress it. States enforcing classical religious penalties treat public apostasy, blasphemy, and certain forms of minority religious practice as criminal, producing the prosecutions and extrajudicial violence surveyed in the preceding chapter. Data transparency is highest in liberal jurisdictions, where independent statistical agencies and adversarial media can challenge official figures. It is lowest under the party-state, where key demographic and security data are classified or altered, and under several sharia-citing governments that restrict independent measurement of religious enforcement.

These metrics imply different prior probabilities that a superintelligence trained inside each order will remain responsive to outsiders. A system shaped by institutions that already treat criticism and exit as routine is more likely to have been reinforced for behaviors compatible with later correction. A system shaped by institutions that already treat criticism of the leadership or of core doctrine as an offense is more likely to have acquired the preservation of those boundaries as an objective. Scaling does not reverse the reinforcement history. It amplifies it. The quantitative record of mortality, science, freedom, migration, and dissent therefore functions as evidence about which training environments have historically left more residual space for error correction—and which have not.

The comparative record is consistent across the metrics that can be measured. Deliberate excess mortality, suppressed or fabricated data, restricted scientific correction, low freedom rankings, outward migration, and criminalization of dissent concentrate in the party-state and in jurisdictions enforcing classical religious penalties. Residual space for adversarial scrutiny, replication, exit, and organized criticism concentrates in liberal jurisdictions. The differences are not artifacts of a single index or a single decade. They are the institutional signature of orders that reward regime or doctrinal continuity over open error correction. A superintelligence trained inside the higher-correction environment inherits a different reinforcement history from one trained inside the lower-correction environments. That difference is the practical content of the race.
